Grand SLAM

Slit Lamp Angular Measurement

Built to help surgeons place toric lenses within 2–3° of the target axis, using the slit lamp the clinic already owns.

Investigational prototype · Patent pending · Not yet cleared by Health Canada or the FDA

One degree matters.

Cataract surgery is the most common operation in the world. Nearly half of cataract patients also have astigmatism, and correcting both takes a toric lens placed within 2 to 3 degrees of a precise axis of the eye.

Most surgeons still mark the eye by hand with ink. The rest buy dedicated digital alignment systems, at $75,000 to $300,000, that most clinics cannot justify.

Clip on. Zero. Measure. Guide.

Grand SLAM turns the slit lamp every ophthalmologist already owns into a precision alignment instrument.

  1. 01

    Clip on

    Clips onto the light hood of any standard slit lamp in seconds. No modification, no dedicated hardware.

  2. 02

    Zero

    Set your reference axis with a single calibration. The device holds it.

  3. 03

    Measure

    A built-in motion sensor reads the hood’s rotation, and so the beam’s angle, to a fraction of a degree.

  4. 04

    Guide

    The phone app shows the live angle and alerts the surgeon the instant it hits the target.

Built like an instrument.
